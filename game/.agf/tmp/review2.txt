FAIL level_design: level-design review 6.4/10 (bar 8), 8 level(s) below 6/10: The ladder has a good teach-then-combine structure and clear intents. Weaknesses: a trivial opening (about 20 levels at 80-99% win rate), a late cliff (L58-L60 at 43-49% casual), fixed hands that bypass the collection meta, heavy card and rival reuse, rule stacking that is too dense, data errors on some cards, and IP risk from near-real names. About half the units are solid, a few are memorable bosses, and many are filler. Curve: Strong mechanic ladder: stat pick, then answering, then tie pot, counting, Super Over, stances, Free Hit, Review, Powerplay, format weights, captain, fog, wildcard, mega pot, chases. Each chapter teaches before it combines, and each ends on a boss (L10, L20, L30, L40, L50, L60).

Problems:
1. Difficulty is front-loaded wrongly. Ch1-2 win rates are 80-99% for casual players, so the first 20 levels barely test anything. Ch5-6 then drop to 43-62% casual (L58 47%, L59 43%, L60 49%). That is a cliff for a 2-minute casual game, and some units also fail skilled players (L35 76% skilled).
2. Mechanic density is too high. Ch3-6 add a new rule about every 2 levels, and stacking (L29, L52, L60) turns the game into a rules lawyer's puzzle, not a quick duel. Players can't hold fog, wildcard, mega pot, formats and captain in their heads at once.
3. Deck reuse and repetition. The same cards (Taijull Islam, Mohammeed Shami, Imaad Waseem, Babbar Azam, Pathirane) appear in nearly every unit. Rival decks are fixed lists of 6 weak or strong cards, so levels feel samey and the 480-card collection never shows.
4. The player's hand is given, not built. That undercuts the Deck Builder, albums and Form upgrades, which are the main retention pillars. Hands should come from the player's own deck, with the given hand only in teaching levels.
5. Breather placement is inconsistent. L7, L17, L27, L37, L47 and L55 are well-timed, but L55 sits before the hardest chase block and is also a very easy 100% skilled win. L58-L60 have no breather after them.
6. Retention: rewards scale linearly (46 to 500 coins) and the only differentiators are the boss captain cards. There is no replay value or star-chasing beyond the 3-star goals, and no variant nodes, so after 60 nodes the loop depends on the meta-layer.
7. Data errors: many cards have bad or inconsistent data. Najibullaah Zadran and Hinerik Klaassen show Economy values with W0, which is impossible. Aandray Russell's ODI and T20 lines are inconsistent, and Test lines for pacers like Shaheen Afridy show unrealistic numbers.
8. The "altered spelling" cartoon names (Babbar Azam, Rohith Sharma, Viraat Kohli) are still obviously real people. This is a licensing and IP risk.
9. Player-facing complexity: aggression, safe, power cards, formats and captains all share the swipe and tap controls. Fog hides the opponent's card but the call gives it away, so it's a weak mystery.

Overall: a clever, teachable design with a good chapter shape, but the difficulty curve is inverted (trivial early, brutal late) and units feel like puzzles with fixed hands.
