FAIL level_design: level-design review 7.3/10 (bar 8), 0 level(s) below 6/10: This is a strong, unusually well-engineered puzzle ladder. Each unit has a stated "aha", a hand built to prove it, and a bot-validated win-rate spread (casual vs skilled) that shows real skill expression. Introducing one mechanic at a time, then combining, is mostly well done. The standout units are L34 (captain on the card that is "just short"), L36/L49 (blank format lines), L38/L39 (format flips), the L57 "unlearn the T20 chase" and the L9/L18 bluff reads.

The set falls short of the EA FC Mobile / Top Eleven standard in six ways.
1. Roughly 15 units are repeats in disguise. They are "locked Strike Rate double" (L11, L15, L17, L55), "sure ball plus a power card" (L22, L23, L27, L47) and "cautious or sandbag rival" (L16 vs L18). Their aha is solved on sight, so there is no real decision.
2. Two breathers (L37, L47) are not breathers. L37 asks for 5 boundaries in 6 balls, and L47 sits on a 351-branch decision space.
3. Several teach units are harsh for a first exposure. L24 loses 3 of 12 competent-bot re-deals, and L33, L41 and L43 sit at 63-69% casual or mid-skill win rates. The pre-validated "teach" promise is broken.
4. The late game (L48-L60) drops to 54-58% mid-skill win rates while stacking 4-6 rules at once (fog + wildcard + mega pot + captain + powerplay + review). Difficulty comes from rule noise and fog-guessing rather than new decisions. That hurts a 2-minute casual audience.
5. Retention rests almost entirely on a fixed-hand puzzle campaign. The 480-card collection, albums, form tiers and deck builder barely touch ladder play, because fixture squads are the "proven default" and only 2 swaps are allowed. Once a unit is solved it has no replay value beyond stars. "Trap rate" is unmeasured ("?") on 55 of 60 units, so uniqueness of the solution cannot be verified.
6. The mid-game rule ramp is heavy. Plain T20 → weighted T20 → ODI → Test → captain lands in CH4 with three consecutive teaches. A bigger rule is also hidden: "plain T20 (no weights)" in CH1-3 versus "weights ON" later changes what the same stat is worth.

Smaller issues:
- The coin curve is erratic (L7 97, L8 148, L9 114, L10 200).
- Rival AI skill is non-monotone (L60 final 0.88 < L59 0.94 and L58 0.92).
- The L60 final omits Free Hit and Powerplay.
- L59 claims a rival Review that the rules line does not list.
- The L35 and L51 intent texts contain factual contradictions (details in those entries). Curve: Structure: 6 chapters of 10 nodes. Each has a TEACH opener, PRACTISE/COMBINE/TWIST middle, a SPIKE and a BOSS finale, with breathers near L7/L17/L27/L37/L47/L55. This is a clean, readable rhythm.

Mechanic order:
- CH1: stat pick, bands, answering, tie pot, counting, Super Over. Solid, with the Super Over taught alone at L5.
- CH2: Aggressive (L11), Safe (L13), chase (L19), sandbag (L18). Fine.
- CH3: Free Hit (L21), Review (L24), Powerplay (L27), then triple stacks (L29/L30). Each is taught alone first, so this is good.
- CH4: format weights (L31-L33) and captain (L34-L35). Three format teaches in a row is a big ramp, and "weights" quietly changes the maths of the first 30 levels.
- CH5: fog, wildcard, mega pot, which stack from L46 on. L45 (mega pot) and L48 (fog + mega pot) add rules whose payoff is rare because dead-heats only occur between non-bowlers or blank lines.
- CH6: mixes, then three hard chases (L56-L58), a semi (L59) and a final (L60).

Difficulty:
- Casual win rate falls from ~89% to ~55%, and mid-skill bot win rate from ~91% to 54-58%.
- Good: the casual-to-skilled gap widens (L60 54% vs 94%), so skill is visible.
- Bad: CH5-6 for casuals is near coin-flip. Combined with no stated retry, energy or hint plan, it risks a wall at roughly L45+.
- The L10 boss (79% casual) is easier than L8 (76%) and uses an untaught two-over rule.
- The spikes (L8, L18, L28, ...) are well placed and pay more, but the reward curve is not smooth.

Retention gaps:
- Ladder hands are fixed, so collecting, form tiers and deck building are almost irrelevant to the 60-node path, which is solvable by lookup.
- 3rd-star rewards give the rival star card (one of ~60 cards) plus cosmetics only.
- The ladder needs a reason to replay: scaled rematches ("Elite" versions with swapped hands and your own deck allowed), a limited optimization/par-score star, and rival-captain card upgrades.
- Daily Chase, Weekly Format Cup and Season Track are listed but unspecified here, so they must carry long-term retention.
- The reuse of ~100 cards across 60 rivals will feel familiar by CH4; rotate the pool by country/era per chapter.
- The finale should fully mirror the L30/L40 pattern (all power cards, a rival Powerplay) and have the highest AI skill.

L10 (6/10): Introduces the two-over aggregate for the first time inside a boss with no teach, and is easier (79% casual) than L8/L9. Not a mastery test; 12 balls also breaks the 2-minute promise. → Teach two-over aggregate in a prior short unit (L9 or a new one), and make the boss test counting plus the L5 Super Over tiebreak with a level 12-ball finish.
L15 (6/10): Dominant strategy: all four of your hitters beat their entire hand on SR. It is L11 again with weaker payoffs (they Safe) and no decision. → Let one hitter (e.g. Mitchell 138.3) lose to Shadaab 137.0 only if Hasaranga is spent, creating a count-dependent swipe.
L16 (6/10): Same idea as L18 (cheap answers to Safe calls, save aces); bot wins 29-6 so hardly any tension. → Merge the cautious-rival concept into L18 and use L16 for something new (e.g. introduce the rival who switches from Safe to Aggressive on ball 5).
L17 (6/10): Third 'locked double' unit (L11, L15, L17); 38-0 bot score. Nothing to decide. → Make the 14-run margin require sequencing: only one sure SR double per hitter, with the miser's Economy double available only after a rival miser is spent.
L22 (6/10): The aha is the obvious stack and should have been L21's lesson; bot margin 36-26 with 71% casual means players just guess the ball. → Fold into L21, and use this slot to test choosing between Free Hit on a six-band ball vs saving it for a risky second call.
L24 (6/10): Competent bot fails 3/12 re-deals (9/12), unacceptable for a teach unit; the 'save for boundary loss' lesson isn't guaranteed to be demonstrated. → Rebuild the fixed squad so a Review ball arises on any line of play (an unavoidable 4-run loss on ball 2) and raise re-deal success to 12/12.
L25 (6/10): Competent bot only 14-9 and 9/12 re-deals; 69% casual. Review timing is heavily luck of the deal because rival aggression depends on AI draw. → Fix the gambler's order of aggressive calls in a seeded script, give your hand one more mid-card, and demand 12/12 re-deals.
L27 (6/10): Powerplay's double-edged nature (doubles their ball too) is never felt; 96% mid-skill means a teach with no risk. → Put one rival threat on the next call so a careless Powerplay costs 6+ runs, then break out the safe path.
L31 (6/10): Opens CH4 with a gambler AI teaching a major rule change; 'plain T20' in CH1-3 vs 'weights on' later is unexplained, and 71% casual win is harsh for a teach. → Use a balanced AI, add a Rules chip explaining x1.5, and relabel earlier units as 'basic' to make the shift intentional.
L37 (6/10): Not a breather: five boundaries in six balls is a demanding star, and a Kallis trap plus captain maths adds complexity. → Lower the 3rd star to 3 boundaries, keep the captain-for-band idea, and cut the trap to a single visible one.
L46 (6/10): Stacks two rules without a new insight; 59% casual and 363 choices make it noise rather than depth. → Make the aha concrete: the fog hides a rival with a decisive stat, and the wildcard fix turns a coin-flip into a sure answer.
L47 (6/10): 351-branch decision space on a 'breather'; stars (2 boundaries + 10 run win) are trivial so the wildcard is unnecessary. → Make the 3rd star require a wildcard-created boundary so the new toy is the spectacle.
L51 (6/10): Intent is factually off: 'one of their three ODI batters' ignores Omarzai (38.5) and Shakib (37.3) who also have high Average, and De Kock/Sharma beat all six anyway. Reads as a vanilla ODI unit. → Correct the hand: three clear batters vs three bowlers so Average = batter and Wickets = bowler, and make the answer non-trivial.
L55 (6/10): Another locked-double breather (cf. L17); 91% mid-skill and Review rarely needed. → Use the breather for spectacle: a rival with one ace that makes the captain boost choice matter and the Review a real option.
L1 (7/10): Pure win-more: bot wins 28-0, every batting stat wins, so the only 'decision' is picking the widest gap. Fine as a tutorial but no tension. → Keep one planted trap: one rival tail-ender with a surprisingly high Runs figure (e.g. Starc R99 vs your Runs) so a careless Runs call loses, proving 'pick the widest gap'.
L2 (7/10): The cheapest-winner lesson is clear, but a 19-3 bot score means nearly any answer works; no real cost for misplaying. → Make one rival call a stat only your 'expensive' card can answer, so over-spending early visibly costs ball 5/6.
L4 (7/10): Intent describes 'two mid hitters' but your hand has Marsh 133.8, Livingstone 145.4 and Shaanaka 123.1 (three), and Shaanaka loses to everyone; the counting story is muddy. → Rework the hand to exactly two big hitters, two mid hitters, two non-hitters, and mention which loses to whom on the coach banner.
L6 (7/10): Overlaps L3 (tie pot) and L2 (answering); 'lose small' is the same lesson as L8/L9 a few nodes later. → Make the pot decisive: the over is level except for the pot, forcing the player to engineer the tie rather than merely discover it.
L11 (7/10): Strike Rate is a locked double, so the only choice is 'swipe up'; the wicket-limit risk is never felt. → Give the rival one hitter at SR 125+ so one call is genuinely unsafe and the player must read the coach banner.
L12 (7/10): Intent says exactly two 30+ Average anchors per family, but Inglys (30.16), Kohli and Amla make three. Counting rule is stated incorrectly. → Lower Inglys's Average family or change the intent to 'three 30+', and re-run the proof.
L13 (7/10): Safe is introduced two units after Aggressive, so it feels like a footnote; the lesson depends on knowing exactly how Normal differs. → Show a side-by-side 'Normal / Safe / Aggressive' payoff preview on the shaky call, and teach Safe before or with Aggressive.
L21 (7/10): Free Hit is a free win with no cost; the 'where to spend it' decision is real, but the 36-22 bot score shows low stakes. → Include a rival with a trap card (a mid hitter that beats your riskiest call), so the player must identify the one true risky ball.
L23 (7/10): Same decision as L22 with a wicket twist; trap rate 0 so the optimal line is easy; 70% casual is a bit harsh for a practise unit. → Rival gambler swipes up on every card that beats a threshold; make the insurance ball visible in the deck preview.
L28 (7/10): Competent bot wins 25-0, meaning the rival's Powerplay doesn't bite against competent play; the spike is only felt by casuals (65%). → Rebalance the hand so the rival Powerplay threatens a swing even against the competent bot (target 18-12 scoreline).
