FAIL level_design: level-design review 7/10 (bar 8), 1 level(s) below 6/10: The set is well structured and teaches one mechanic at a time, with strong "aha" lines and proof data. It has some weak units: duplicate-feeling early drills, near-autowin breathers, an opaque late-game chase and format stack, and difficulty spikes in chapters 3, 5 and 6, topped by an unfair final. Curve: Strong spine: mechanics are introduced one at a time (margin bands, answering, tie pot, counting, Super Over, aggressive, safe, Free Hit, Review, Powerplay, formats, captain, fog, wildcard, mega pot), then combined in a boss at the end of each chapter, and the six-chapter, 60-node shape is clear. Almost every unit states a concrete "aha", and the proof lines show the puzzles are solvable.

Problems:
1. Teaching density is front-loaded and the middle sags. L1-L10 are mostly "pick the best stat" lessons with 89-99% skilled win rates. L7, L17, L27, L37, L47 and L55 are scripted breathers placed almost exactly every 10 units, so the rhythm is predictable.
2. The late curve is brutal. Chapter 5 and 6 casual win rates are 50-59%, with L60 at 42% for the mid-skill bot and L48 and L50 at 54%. That is a wall for casual players, with no stated soft landing such as assist, retry, hint or card-upgrade gating. Card Form and the deck swaps are meant to be the difficulty valve, but the proof uses fixed squads, so nothing shows that upgraded decks help.
3. Difficulty steps are lumpy. Chapter 3 jumps from L22 at 71% to L23 at 70%, L26 at 74%, then L28 at 65%. L30 is a boss with a 59% mid-skill win rate, harder than the chapter 4 boss L40 at 69%. L36 and L37 are inverted: the hard Test blank twist sits just before a breather that has a 99% win rate and is effectively a free pass.
4. Format changes are introduced late (L31, chapter 4). Cards read as T20 only for 30 units, so chapter 4 feels like a second game. Fog and wildcard are then stacked on formats with too little room between lessons.
5. Hard chases (L56-L58) are labelled PRACTISE and SPIKE but sit in the final chapter, after the breather L55. There are three chase-only units in a row with different tricks, and L57 is closer to a coin flip (30-32 on the first deal).
6. Retention hooks are thin inside this content. Every unit awards 3rd-star "their star card", which is a good chase, but there is no replay variance because the squads are fixed. Rewards escalate unevenly (L8 gives 148 coins against 114 for L9, and L18 gives 208). The 3rd stars are mostly "win 4+ balls", "no wickets lost" or "boundaries", repeated 60 times, so the three-star ladder is flat. There is no star-gated chapter unlock and no visible mastery chain.
7. Fairness and clarity are unproven. Many "optimal moves: > search cap" and "trap rate: ?" lines mean the solver never confirmed a unique best line. The player must also see the rival's hand to count, so the UI aids (beats-X-of-N chips) risk doing the thinking for them.
8. Format weights make the arithmetic opaque (x1.5 stacks with x2, and Powerplay x2 stacks to x4). The 1.5x rule varies by format. I would test whether the margin bands are readable.

Recommended pacing changes: add a format teaser around L21-25. Cap boss difficulty at about 70% for the mid-skill bot in chapters 3 to 5. Place breathers after hard units rather than on a fixed cadence. Add variant hands, such as a re-deal with the same lesson, so the 60 units have replay value.
