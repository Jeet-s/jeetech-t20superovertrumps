Every unit has a stated lesson, solved fixtures, and a mechanic introduced alone before it is combined. Rival archetypes (balanced, cautious, gambler, baiter, aggro) give variety. The recurring weaknesses are these. (1) Most units are the same puzzle: find the stat that beats all six, hold it, concede the rest. (2) Many fixtures only have one clean solution, so they read as a worksheet. (3) Win rates fall to 49-60% casual in CH5-6 while the rewards stay flat, so the cliff is steep for casuals. (4) Several aha lines have to be told to the player, because the on-screen coach is missing or vague. (5) Some units repeat an earlier one with a new coat: L44/L47, L12/L11, L26/L19/L56. (6) The meta hook is thin. There is nothing on the ladder about seasons, rivals with names, or re-playable depth beyond stars. The stars loop into albums, which is good. Fairness is mostly fine, but a few units look overtuned: L20, L49, L60, L28 and L58 have skilled bots at 61-66%, and the proof lines show close margins (48-41, 24-24). Unit-level notes are below for anything under 8. Curve: Chapters 1-2 ramp well: stat pick, answering, tie pot, card counting, super over, then aggressive and safe stances. L7 and L17 are real breathers. Chapter 3 has the best sequencing (Free Hit, Review, Powerplay, each taught alone, then stacked in L29/L30). Chapter 4 introduces three format weights and the captain in a row. L31-L33 come too close together, and L34 then drops the weights, which is jarring. Chapter 5 front-loads mechanics: fog, wildcard, mega pot, bluff. Chapter 6 then recombines them, but L51-L60 are mostly stacks of rules rather than new ideas. Difficulty is not smooth. Casual win rates go 100/94/93/91 early, 77 at L20, 58-60 by L49-L56, and 52 at L60, while reward growth is linear (46 to 500 coins). Boss rewards (L10, L20, L30, L40, L50, L60) work as milestones, but only the final one feels like a finale. The hardest content comes at 4-5x the early win rate drop with no catch-up or retry help. Breathers are inconsistent: L37, L44, L47 and L55 are fine, but L47 is still a 65-77% duel that follows a hard unit. There is no power-up or deck-upgrade gating, so the ladder never says why a player should upgrade cards or build a better deck. Squad swaps of 2 are the only link between the meta and the ladder. Return reasons are albums, packs, the daily chase and streaks, which are solid but off-ladder. Recommend a replay track (harder modifiers on cleared nodes), named rival story beats per chapter, and a deck-power ramp that matches the casual curve.
