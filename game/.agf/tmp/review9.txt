FAIL level_design: level-design review 6.5/10 (bar 8), 0 level(s) below 6/10: The set is a polished, well-sequenced tutorial ladder of authored perfect-information puzzles, with one new rule per few units and a boss at each chapter end. Weaknesses are heavy repetition of the 'find the sure ball' solution and no real hidden-information duel until chapter 5. Difficulty is flat for skilled players and spikes for casuals. There is no evident link between the fixed squads and the collection meta. Several late units are rule soups. Many units rate as solid but forgettable, and a few have real design issues. The strongest are the boss and twist units with new spectacle: L10, L20, L38, L40, L53 and L60. Curve: Strengths: the 60-unit ladder has a clear mechanic-per-unit teaching spine. Stat pick and bands come first (L1-L10), then stances (L11-L20), then Free Hit, Review and Powerplay (L21-L30), then format weights and captain (L31-L40), then fog, Wildcard and Mega Pot (L41-L50), then combined chases and finals (L51-L60). Each chapter ends in a boss, and breathers are placed on a regular cadence. The rewards escalate, and rival captain-cards, pitches and emotes give unlock hooks.

Problems:
1. Difficulty is front-loaded then flat-lined. Casual win rates are 100/94/93/91 in ch1, but by ch3 to ch6 they sit at 55-75% with mid-skill bot wins of 55-60% and no gentle ramp. Skilled bots win about 90-95% all the way through, so the ladder never gets harder for experts. It only gets more complicated.
2. The teach/practise/twist formula repeats in every chapter with the same shape: solve a puzzle by finding the 'sure' cards. This is a perfect-information logic puzzle, not a duel. The deck preview shows the rival's whole hand, so most units have a fixed, dominant line (the 'aha'). Replay value is low once solved, and it fits poorly with the 'Marvel Snap / Clash Royale' positioning, which depends on hidden information and meta shifts. Fog of war only arrives at L41.
3. Fixed fixture squads (the proof uses a specific squad) make the ladder a set of authored puzzles. They do not connect to the collection or Form-tier meta in the doc. Collection value is unclear, because teach units fix the squad and the rest only allow a 2-card swap.
4. Chapters 4 to 6 pile rules on rules. By L58-L60, players juggle weights, formats, stances, fog, Wildcard, pots, captain, Review and Powerplay with no new reward than more rules. Cognitive load may exceed the 2-minute casual fix promised to a 13-year-old audience.
5. Rewards are flat. Coins climb roughly linearly, and the same pack tiers repeat. There is no obvious reason to return after the 60 nodes beyond the daily chase, which the content set does not define. Missing: repeatable or randomised rival hands, a endless/rotating mode, and a way for players' own decks to matter.
6. Several mid-ladder boss stars reward luck or are hard to read (L30, L50, L60 at 54-63% casual).
7. Data hazards: Test and ODI blanks (L36, L49) feel like punishing rules. Many units reuse the same cards (Salt, Surya, Kohli, de Kock) and the same sentence template, so the set feels samey.
