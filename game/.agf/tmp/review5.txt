FAIL level_design: level-design review 7.2/10 (bar 8), 1 level(s) below 6/10: This is a strong, well-engineered puzzle ladder. Each unit has a stated lesson, and the mechanics are introduced one at a time: stat pick, answering a call, tie pot, stances, Free Hit, Review, formats, captain, fog, Wildcard, Mega Pot, then chases and combined bosses. Several units are very good: L34 (captain on the 'just short' card), L38 (format rotation) and L60 (the final). Most of the rest are solid.

Weaknesses:
1. Several intents are not true of the setup. The data contradicts the stated aha in L8, L9, L13, L14, L35, L39, L56 and L59. These are fixable by swapping one or two cards.
2. Several teach units are won so easily that the lesson is optional: L27 (bot wins 51-4), L28 (25-0) and the 99–100% breathers. A teach unit needs a way to lose if you ignore the mechanic.
3. Third stars are formulaic. About 40 of 60 are 'no wickets lost', which has nothing to do with the unit's lesson and is meaningless where there is no wicket limit (L55 Review 'wicket back'). Star-card rewards repeat too: Rizwaan 3x, Buttler 4x, Kohli 3x.
4. Rewards climb a flat +6 coins per level, with no surprises.
5. The headline mechanic (Aggressive/Safe swipe) first appears at L11, so chapter 1 is about 25 minutes of pure stat-picking. That is a slow hook for a 2-minute-fix game.
6. Fixture squads make each node a solved puzzle. Combined with 'swap 2 cards' and Form boosts of up to +5%, the collection barely matters in PvE, and there is little replay once a node has 3 stars. The meta (albums, packs) is disconnected from the ladder.
7. Trap rate is '?' on almost every unit, and 0 on the chases and L23. The skill ceiling and the number of tempting wrong moves are unmeasured.
8. Placeholder-looking stats (several R1000 lines: Klaassen, Naib, Shanto, Taijul) break the 'every number is real' promise.
9. The 3-over final and the 2-over bosses run well past the '2 minute' positioning.
10. Altered-spelling names of recognisable real cricketers are a likeness and takedown risk. The studio-bar answer is a FICA/ICC licence or fully fictional archetypes.
11. Some rules are underspecified: what happens at 'all out', whether Wildcard swaps or lends, and whether Review replays the ball. Curve: Rival skill rises almost monotonically (0.20 to 0.95). Casual win rate falls from about 89% to 50% and skilled from 99% to 82%. This is a clean ramp with a boss every 10 units (L10, L20, L30, L40, L50), a semi-final and a final. Mechanics are introduced alone and then combined, and breathers follow hard units (L7, L17, L27, L37, L47, L55).

Problems:
- Chapter 1 is too long and too passive: 10 units with no stances. The swipe, which is the signature input, arrives at L11. Move Aggressive/Safe to about L4–5 and cut the ch1 stat-pick drills.
- Skilled players win 95–100% through L1–L21 (L1 99, L2 99, L3 99, L10 100, L17 100, L27 99). That is half the game with almost no tension for good players. Raise rival skill or tighten hands in ch1–2.
- Difficulty is dishonest in a few places. Bots win 25-0 (L28), 51-4 (L27), 38-0 (L17) and 44-1 (L37) while casual players still lose 35% on spike units. That suggests the bot is not a good proxy for a human reading the UI. Add a human-like (noisy-information) bot to validation.
- Chases are overused: L19, L26, L56, L57 and L58 are the same shape with a different number. Stack three of them at the end of chapter 6, right before the semi-final, and the finale arrives with a feeling of repetition. Space them out (one per chapter from ch2) and give each a different twist.
- Format teaching is compressed. T20, ODI and Test weights get one teach unit each in L31–33. Format rotation (L38, L40, L54) appears before players have practised ODI alone. Add one ODI or Test practise unit.
- Chapters 4–6 pile on systems (fog, Wildcard, Mega Pot, rotation, captain, Review, Powerplay). The late game is combinatorial rather than one-idea-per-unit. The UI needs per-ball summaries (weights, format, which power is armed) to avoid overload.
- Retention beyond L60 is missing from this content. After the final, nothing is described except the Daily Chase and Weekly Cup. Add a 'Legend' re-run of each chapter (fixed hands, but with the player's own deck and Form boosts) and rotating modifier nodes, so that collection and upgrades matter.
- Reward design is a flat ramp. Add milestone surprises (a themed pitch, an emote, a legendary pack) at 10/20/30/40/50, not just at bosses.
