FAIL level_design: level-design review 7/10 (bar 8), 0 level(s) below 6/10: A cleverly sequenced rules ladder with strong teaching intent, but it plays like a solved-puzzle campaign more than a duel. Fixed hands undercut the collection meta. Difficulty and rule stacking spike in Ch4-6, and many 'breathers' aren't. Several units are near-duplicates of each other. Fixes per unit are below. Units rated 8+ are omitted. Curve: Strengths: a very clean mechanic ladder. Ch1 teaches pick, answer, tie pot, counting, Super Over. Ch2 teaches the stances. Ch3 teaches Free Hit, Review and Powerplay. Ch4 teaches format weights and captain. Ch5 teaches fog, Wildcard and Mega Pot. Ch6 combines them in hard chases and a final. Each chapter has a TEACH, then PRACTISE or TWIST, then BREATHER, then BOSS shape. The chase and Test-chase units (L56-L58) are well-judged mastery tests. Rewards rise steadily and the 3rd-star rival-card hook gives collection pull.

Problems:
1. The curve is too deterministic and puzzle-like. Nearly every unit is solved by a 'sure ball' hint written in the coach text. That reads as a logic puzzle with a single answer, not a competitive duel. Fixed fixture hands (squad fixed or swaps ≤2) sit in tension with the collection, Form tier and deck-building meta. The core fantasy is that your own deck matters, but the campaign makes your collection nearly irrelevant.
2. Difficulty spikes and wobbles. Casual win rates fall from about 90% to 55-65% from L28 onward. That is harsh for a 2-minute mobile game, and the boss and final casual rates (54-63%) will cause churn. L20 and L30 (aggro/gambler bosses) show bot results of 52-34 and 66-52, so they are narrow and high variance. Skilled and casual rates diverge by 30-40 points in many units, so the game is only fair to people who read the coach text.
3. Too many breathers are not breathers. L17, L27, L37, L44 and L47 vary from 74% to 91% casual win rates, and several carry heavy rules (Wildcard, captain, Powerplay).
4. Mechanic overload arrives too early. L22, L29 and L30 stack Powerplay, Aggressive and Free Hit. The pacing of introducing one new rule per unit is fine until about L43, then Ch5/6 add fog, bluffing, Mega Pot, rotation and blanks in quick succession with little consolidation.
5. Every unit is set in one over of 6 balls with similar structure. Variety of spectacle (a target-score unit, a pressure unit, a rivalry narrative) is thin. Mid-chapter units feel like repeated 'count the cards' work.
6. Retention loop is only implied. There is no visible story, no named rival arcs beyond rival captain cards, and no replay value (the stars are fixed puzzle solutions). Add star-gated chapter doors, remixable daily hands from the player's own deck, a rotating 'Rival rematch' with different hands, and a PvP bridge so the ladder feeds the online mode.
7. Data and fairness risks. The Economy '—' rule and blank Test lines are hard to explain on-screen. Unreadable stat displays (Test E 2.8 vs 3.0 differences) and hidden weights make margin bands hard to predict. Real career numbers create a currency-staleness problem, and altered spellings may still draw licensing and likeness risk for real people (Tendulkaar, Dhonni). Add a visible 'run band preview' for every stat, which the aids partly do.
REDESIGN THESE LEVELS (keep the ones not listed):
L7 (6/10): A breather with a 3-star ask (3 boundaries) that is a puzzle, not a relax; near-identical to L1 in feel. → Make 3 stars 'win by 20+' with a clear show-off ball; give a different theme (a big six reel).
L9 (6/10): Mechanically a repeat of L2/L8 (save specialists to answer) with a different costume. → Replace with a unit where the batters-vs-bowlers split is read from a face-down hand, or fold into L8.
L12 (6/10): Reads as a lookup table of aces per stat; high homework, low spectacle. → Surface the 'aces' list on the pre-duel card automatically so the unit tests the decision, not the memorisation.
L13 (6/10): Teach Safe with a rival that lets the player win without it; Safe is rarely the best play in a 6-ball over, so the lesson is weak. → Give a duel where two lost balls would flip the result so Safe clearly saves it; make 3 stars 'win with 2 Safe balls'.
L14 (6/10): Another 'trap card' puzzle with the same tail-end rival as L1/L7; swipe-everything is trivial except one card. → Give the rival one real hitter so some calls are risky; make the two-wicket limit matter.
L16 (6/10): A second Super Over at only 78% skilled win; tie-break (fewer wickets) is a hidden rule. → Show the tie-break on the scoreboard; merge with L5 or move it to Ch3.
L17 (6/10): A 'breather' with a precise 3-star puzzle (Economy ball for 8+ runs); the rival is cautious and has nothing to fear. → Make 3 stars 'win by 24+' and let any route get it.
L18 (6/10): Sandbag rival is a good idea but 72% casual win and a nontrivial read; the player cannot tell sandbagging before it happens. → Show the bandits' lead cards with a visible 'sandbag' tag on the rule chips and a post-ball tracker.
L20 (6/10): An always-aggressive boss with a 3-wicket limit and a 52-34 bot margin: very swingy, 73% casual. Two overs mean the rival's second hand is an unrelated six. → Lower the skill and give the player one Safe-friendly card; tell the player which cards the rival leads with.
L22 (6/10): A trivial combination: Free Hit plus Aggressive is a no-brainer on a sure ball, so the 'twist' is just a bigger number. → Make the sure ball dependent on a rival swap or fog read so the pair has a cost.
L24 (6/10): Review taught with a 3-star 'no wickets lost' that rarely uses Review; the lesson is 'don't use it on a single', which is passive. → Engineer a hand where one forced loss is a boundary and make the star require using Review on it.
L28 (6/10): Rival Powerplay is a surprise, 65% casual win and a bot sweep of 25-0, so it is either trivial or punishing. → Telegraph the rival Powerplay with a glowing icon the ball before it fires; keep the Review hint.
L29 (6/10): Stacking three powers on a locked call is a spreadsheet exercise; the 24-run ball is a one-note puzzle and 44-36 bot margin shows a swingy duel. → Add a rival counter so the stack can be punished, and cap the 3-star at a 16+ ball.
L31 (6/10): Format weights are introduced at the same time as a gambler rival, so the player cannot tell the lesson from the opponent. → Show a weighted-run preview on each stat row and use a balanced rival.
L32 (6/10): ODI weights is a stat-value shuffle; players mostly see bigger numbers, little new decision. → Put the 1.5x badge on the stat rows and make the 3-star a call on Average with a margin band.
L33 (6/10): Test weights with all rival Test legends gives a 68% casual win; the lesson is 'avoid Runs' which is negative. → Balance the rivals with a few weak Test cards and require the player to find the sure Average calls.
L35 (6/10): Captain plus Test weights plus a cautious rival: the stacking hides the lesson and the bot margin is 14-11, thin. → Drop Test weights here and use them in L37.
L36 (6/10): Blank lines are a confusing twist, 10/12 re-deals with a 68% mid bot; the real-world stat gap (a card without Test career) will feel like a bug. → Display an 'DNP' badge with an explainer tooltip and keep half the blanks on the rival side.
L42 (6/10): Fog plus aggressive plus wicket limit with a 55% mid bot win rate; punishing. → Lower the skill to 0.60 and show a loud-call tracker.
L44 (6/10): Labelled breather but still a puzzle; it duplicates L43 on the answering side. → Make it a relaxed Wildcard score attack with a visible winner.
L46 (6/10): Fog plus Wildcard with a long read chain; the 122 starting choices make the 'one decision per ball' claim false. → Add a candidate-card narrowing aid and split into two simpler units.
L47 (6/10): Called a breather but has a hidden Buttler trap and a boundary-making Wildcard requirement. → Remove the trap and make the 3-star a simple win by 10+.
L48 (6/10): Fog plus Mega Pot plus stance: a three-way reading tax at 64% casual. → Remove fog or stances; keep the pot headline.
L49 (6/10): Blank Test cards plus Wildcard is a niche trick at a 55% casual win rate, the lowest of any non-final unit. → Shorten the hand to 4 Test cards and a hint overlay; raise casual to ~65%.
L52 (6/10): Another Mega Pot unit that adds Powerplay; pot-feeding logic is subtle and it overlaps L45/L48. → Cut the pot lesson and make this a pure Powerplay x Mega Pot showpiece.
